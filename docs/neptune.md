# Neptune sources and rendering

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun. Its atmosphere carries methane-ice clouds in extremely powerful winds, reaching over 2,000 kilometres per hour. Neptune has no solid visible surface.

- [NASA Neptune facts](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/neptune-facts/)
- [NASA Neptune fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/neptunefact.html)

This World is a procedural cinematic interpretation, not spacecraft imagery or a physical atmospheric simulation. The default Cinematic blue palette draws on enhanced Voyager imagery; corrected visible-light imagery is paler and more blue-green, represented by the Natural blue-green palette. Deep azure and Neutral survey provide alternative grades.

The slightly oblate globe has subtle cloud bands, white methane-cloud streaks and a localized dark vortex with swirling companion clouds. The storm is inspired by Neptune's transient dark spots, not a claim that Voyager's Great Dark Spot still exists. Latitude-dependent jets move relative to the rotating globe. A default globe circuit lasts 15 minutes and the cloud motion is accelerated for wallpaper viewing; neither represents the actual rotation period or measured wind speeds.

Cloud detail controls small cloud structure, Camera drift adjusts gentle framing movement, Cloud drift controls differential winds and storm swirl, and Atmosphere adjusts the narrow scattering limb without removing the opaque cloud deck. Movement zero freezes time and audio response. Audio subtly modulates the planet's brightness, leaving the stationary sky unchanged. Brightness zero blacks out the complete composition.

The stars reuse the shared NASA Milky Way and catalog-star asset; see `assets/space/ATTRIBUTION.md`. No new Neptune texture assets are used. Moons and rings are omitted from this planet-focused composition.
