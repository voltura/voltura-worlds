# Venus sources and rendering

Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System. Its thick, mostly carbon-dioxide atmosphere traps heat, and opaque sulfuric-acid clouds hide the surface. Cloud patterns are more distinct in ultraviolet images than in visible light.

- [NASA Venus facts](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/)
- [NASA Hubble Venus cloud tops](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/venus-cloud-tops/)

This World is a procedural artistic reconstruction, not a spacecraft texture or atmospheric simulation. Cream and pale gold cloud decks, latitude-dependent shear, retrograde atmospheric motion, subdued visible-light contrast, and a narrow warm scattering limb convey the cloud-covered planet. Motion is accelerated to a 15-minute cloud circuit at default Movement; it does not represent the 243-day solid-body rotation period. The opaque cloud deck remains present at every Atmosphere setting. Surface heat is not depicted as visible lava or emission through the clouds.

The star background reuses the shared NASA Milky Way and catalog-star asset; see `assets/space/ATTRIBUTION.md`. No additional Venus textures are required.
